Product Roadmap

What we’re building next.

A transparent look at what’s live, what’s next, and what we’re exploring as Billboard grows from bill summaries into a deeper civic information tool.

This roadmap is organized by suggested build order, not dates. Priorities can shift as we learn what creates the clearest value for users.

Last reviewed: November 12, 2026

Starting point

Billboard’s first job is clarity: AI-powered summaries, real-world context on why each bill matters, bill tracking, and shareable bill pages.

Available Now

The core loop is live.

Billboard already helps users read bills, follow what matters to them, vote, and compare results with how Congress voted.

  • Plain-language summaries Every bill gets a plain-language summary of what it does and why it matters, written with AI from the official bill text and labelled that way.
  • Voting on bills Vote Yea, Nay or Abstain on any bill and add your reasons if you like.
  • Results after you vote See how everyone voted, how people with a verified district voted, and how Congress voted. Results show only combined counts, and a state or district breakdown appears only after enough people there have voted.
  • Topics, life areas and members Follow topics, life areas such as taxes, housing and student loans, and members of Congress.
  • Bill alerts and the Weekly Brief Watch a bill to hear when it moves, and catch up with the Weekly Brief.
  • Your district and your Congress Find your district from your address, see your senators and representative, and follow any member.
  • Every outcome Each bill shows where it stands: new, updated, passed, failed or became law.

Next Up

Keep up with Congress.

The next work follows the new Congress and makes older bills easier to read.

  • The 120th Congress Bills from the 120th Congress, which begins on January 3, 2027.
  • Bills from past Congresses Clearer handling of bills from past Congresses, the Congress shown on bill numbers, and the new House maps.
  • Open source Publishing Billboard’s code as open source.

After That

Deepen context.

Once the core experience is stronger, Billboard can add broader views of district-level patterns.

  • District and state reports Free reports that sum up bills, official votes, and issue movement for each state and district, built from official data, not from Billboard users’ votes.

Longer-Term Bets

Build a useful civic data layer.

Longer-term work could make Billboard’s data and tools useful to researchers, newsrooms, classrooms, and civic groups, not only to people using the app.

  • Open data access Exploring free, documented access to bill summaries and official bill data, with fair-use limits. It would not include Billboard users’ votes.

Neutral

Billboard takes no side on any bill. It has no ads, no paid placement, and no sponsored polls, and we do not sell personal information.

Billboard is an independent project and is not affiliated with Congress or any government agency.
